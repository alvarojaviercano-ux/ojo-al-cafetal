Phase 3 of 6 · CRISP-ML

Model Engineering

Choosing, training and checking the models.

Algorithm selection

Each model from our course answers a different question about a leaf.

K-MEANS

Which part is damaged?

It puts pixels with similar colors into K groups. Green leaf, spots, and background end up in different groups.

Steps: pick K centers. Put each point in the group of the nearest center. Move each center to the middle of its group. Repeat.

LINEAR REGRESSION

How much is damaged?

It draws a line to predict a number: the percent of damage.

LOGISTIC REGRESSION

Healthy or sick?

It gives a number between 0 and 1. If the number is above 0.5, we say the leaf is sick.

KNN

Which disease is it?

K-Nearest Neighbors. It finds the K leaves most like the new one, and picks the disease that shows up the most.

Q-LEARNING

What is the best action?

An agent learns by trying actions and getting rewards. A table keeps a score for each action in each situation.

RANDOM FOREST

Healthy or sick? (used in the app)

Many decision trees vote. It handled the 26 numbers better than the simple models.

Training

  • Split. 80% of the data to train and 20% to test (train_test_split, random_state = 42).
  • Healthy or sick. Logistic Regression, KNN and Random Forest (300 trees) were trained on the 26 numbers.
  • Which disease. KNN and other models were tried on the 6 classes.
  • How much damage. Linear Regression predicts the middle of each rust range: 0, 3, 13, 35.5 and 75%.
  • Where is the damage. K-Means runs on every new photo, on the pixels of the leaf only.
  • What to do. Q-Learning runs in a simulated coffee plot. It learns one table for resistant and one for susceptible varieties.

Validation

I did not use a plain random split only. RoCoLe has 4 photos of each plant, so photos of the same plant in both train and test make the score look better than it is. I also checked every model with a split by plant (GroupKFold).

Hyperparameters

  • Random Forest: 300 trees.
  • K-Means for the damage zone: I compared two color spaces. With red-green-blue the result was dominated by brightness, and the median damage was 0 in every class. With the a and b color channels (Lab) it worked, so I used 5 groups and a vote of 30%: a group counts as damage if at least 30% of its pixels are yellow, orange or brown.
  • Limit: I did not run a full search of settings for every model. The numbers above come from a few careful comparisons, not from an automatic search.
