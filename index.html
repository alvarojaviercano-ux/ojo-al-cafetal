Project title

Ojo al Cafetal

Spot coffee leaf diseases from a photo.

Álvaro Javier Cano · Machine Learning · UDEC · Final project

Try the app →

Problem statement and justification

Coffee is a big part of Colombia. When a coffee plant gets sick, you see it on the leaf first. But we often find the disease too late.

Leaf damage

Rust and red spider mites leave spots and make the leaf weak.

Little expert help

Many farmers have no expert close by to check every plant.

Late detection

When we find the disease late, it spreads and the farmer loses coffee.

Guiding question: can we find the disease with just one photo?

The solution

A simple web page. It checks a photo of a leaf and tells the farmer what to do.

  1. Upload. The user takes a photo of one leaf with a phone and sends it.
  2. Analyze. The model looks at the leaf. It checks the color and the spots.
  3. Receive. The user gets a clear answer: which disease it is, how bad it is, and what to do.

Why it is worth doing

  • It helps the farmer act on time and lose less coffee.
  • It only needs a phone and the internet. No expert visit is needed for a first check.
  • I can do it: I already use Flask, scikit-learn, and Render in my earlier modules, so I do not need new tools.

The name comes from a Colombian saying. “Ojo al cafetal” means “keep an eye on the coffee farm.”

Project objectives

General objective: build a web app that finds coffee leaf diseases from a photo, so farmers can act on time.

Specific objectives

  1. Check that the RoCoLe data is good enough for the project: its quality, its use, and its size.
  2. Prepare the photos: clean them, group the classes, and add my own photos of arabica leaves.
  3. Build and compare the models: K-Means, Linear Regression, Logistic Regression, KNN, and Q-Learning.
  4. Measure how well the models work, with accuracy, precision, and recall. Test them with real photos.
  5. Put the web app online on Render, so farmers can use it from a phone.

Project phases

The work had four phases. Each one led to the next.

  1. Problem and data. I defined the problem: find coffee leaf diseases in time. I chose the RoCoLe dataset (1,560 photos, 390 plants, 6 classes) and checked its quality and its limits, such as robusta coffee and unbalanced classes.
  2. Preparation and features. I cut the leaf from the background and took 26 numbers from each photo: color, spots, and texture. I split the data by plant, so the test is fair.
  3. Models and evaluation. I trained and compared Logistic Regression, KNN, K-Means, Linear Regression, and Q-Learning. The model in the app (Random Forest) reaches 86% with expert masks and 83% with the automatic mask. Naming the disease is harder: about 52%.
  4. Deployment and testing. I trained the model on my computer and put the web app online on Render with Flask. I tested it with photos from outside the dataset and wrote down the limits: other diseases, photos with several leaves, and arabica coffee.

Reference framework

The work and ideas my project is based on.

1. Background: related work

SMARTPHONE APP

Manso et al.

They built a phone app to find coffee leaf miner and rust. It cuts the leaf from the background, finds the damaged area (with thresholds or k-means), and then classifies the damage. It also gives the percent of leaf damaged. They got about 96% accuracy. A white background worked best.

What I take from it: k-means for the damaged area, a percent of damage, and a plain background for the photo.

RUST SEVERITY

Zuñiga Cajas et al. (2025)

They trained thirteen convolutional neural networks to tell how severe the rust is. They say it is a good option when there is little data, but it does not work well yet on other datasets.

What I take from it: severity matters, and I must test the model on photos from other sources.

DATASET

RoCoLe (2019)

Parraga-Alava and colleagues made a public dataset of robusta leaves, with an expert’s labels and rust levels.

What I take from it: this is my main data.

DATASET

JMuBEN

A public dataset of arabica leaves with 22,591 images. It has rust, Cercospora, Phoma, miner, and healthy leaves.

What I take from it: a way to test with arabica. I must check its license first.

3. Theoretical framework: the models

Each model from our course answers a different question about a leaf. Here is how each one works, in simple words.

QuestionModel
Is the leaf healthy or sick?Logistic Regression
Which disease is it?KNN
Which part of the leaf is damaged?K-Means
How much is damaged?Linear Regression
What is the best action?Reinforcement Learning (Q-Learning)
K-MEANS

Which part is damaged?

It puts pixels with similar colors into K groups. Green leaf, spots, and background end up in different groups.

Steps: pick K centers. Put each point in the group of the nearest center. Move each center to the middle of its group. Repeat.

LINEAR REGRESSION

How much is damaged?

It draws a line to predict a number. For example, it can predict the percent of damage from the size of the spots.

LOGISTIC REGRESSION

Healthy or sick?

It gives a number between 0 and 1. If the number is above 0.5, we say the leaf is sick.

KNN

Which disease is it?

K-Nearest Neighbors. It finds the K leaves most like the new one, and picks the disease that shows up the most.

Example with K = 5: if 4 of the 5 closest leaves have rust level 1, the answer is rust level 1.

Q-LEARNING

What is the best action?

An agent learns by trying actions and getting rewards. A table (the Q-table) keeps a score for each action in each situation.

It also thinks about the future, not only the next reward. In my project, the situation is the diagnosis, and the actions are things like “keep watching” or “apply treatment.” I will set the rewards with simple rules.

5. Results

What the models did with the RoCoLe photos. Every number below comes from my own tests.

86%healthy or sick (expert masks)
83%healthy or sick (automatic mask, as in the app)
~52%which disease (best model)

How I measured it

  • Features. From each photo I took 26 numbers: leaf color (RGB and HSV), the share of yellow, orange and brown, the size and number of spots, and texture.
  • Split by plant. RoCoLe has 4 photos of each plant. If photos of the same plant are in both train and test, the score looks better than it is. So I split the data by plant.
  • Same mask as the app. The first models used the expert’s outline of the leaf. The app cuts the leaf by itself (GrabCut), and this gave a different result. I trained again with the automatic mask, so the number matches what the user gets.

The goal was more than 80% for healthy or sick, and the app model reaches it. Telling the diseases apart is much harder with these features. That is why the app does not name the disease; it says if the leaf looks sick.

K-Means: where is the damage?

K-Means groups the pixels of the leaf by color (5 groups, using the a and b color channels). A group counts as damage if most of its pixels are yellow, orange or brown. The app paints these pixels in magenta and gives the percent of the leaf.

Rust level in RoCoLeTypical K-Means result
Level 2 (6–20% by the expert scale)about 4.5%
Level 3 (21–50%)about 7.8%
Level 4 (more than 50%)about 17.1%

The order is right, but K-Means gives lower numbers than the expert scale. It also misses rust level 1. So the app calls it an estimate of the zone with signs of damage, not a measure of damage.

Q-Learning: what to do?

I made a simulation of one coffee plot. The agent can keep watching, improve crop management, or apply fungicide. The rewards follow Cenicafé’s guidance: resistant varieties (about 80% of the planted area) do not need chemical control, and susceptible ones like Caturra should be treated when the rust level grows. The app uses the learned table to give advice by rust level and variety.

Honest note: the simulation numbers (costs and rewards) are my assumptions. The advice is a help, not a diagnosis, and a technician should confirm it.

What does not work well

LIMIT

Other diseases

The model only knows rust and red spider mite. A leaf with white mold or other spots can still be called “sick”.

LIMIT

Messy photos

If there are several leaves in the photo, the automatic cut can include more than one. The app asks for one leaf on a plain background and warns when the cut looks wrong.

LIMIT

Other coffee types

The training photos are robusta. In a few tests with other photos the answers were right, but the confidence was only 60–70%. A real test with arabica is still needed.

LIMIT

Rust level 1

Very early rust is the hardest case: the spots are small, and K-Means often finds almost nothing.
