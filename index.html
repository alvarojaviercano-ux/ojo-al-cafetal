Ojo al Cafetal

Finding coffee leaf diseases from a photo.

Álvaro Javier Cano · Machine Learning · UDEC · Final project

The problem: diseases are found too late

Coffee is part of Colombia’s identity. When a coffee plant gets sick, the damage shows first on the leaf.

Leaf damage

Rust and red spider mite spot and weaken the leaf.

Little expert help

Many farmers do not have an expert nearby to check each plant.

Late detection

If the disease is found late, it spreads and the farmer loses production.

Guiding question: can we find the disease with just one photo?

The name comes from a Colombian saying: “ojo al cafetal” means “keep an eye on the coffee farm.”

The solution

A simple web page that checks a leaf photo and tells the farmer what to do.

  1. Upload. The user sends a photo of one leaf, from a phone.
  2. Analyze. The model checks the leaf. It looks at color and spots.
  3. Receive. The user gets a clear result: the disease, how serious it is, and what to do.
Business goal: help the coffee farmer decide in time, with a simple web tool.

Theory: the models from our course

Each model can answer a different question about a leaf. Here is how each one works, in simple words.

K-MEANS

Which part is affected?

It groups pixels with similar color into K groups. Healthy green, spots, and background fall into different groups.

Steps: pick K centers, assign each point to the nearest center, move each center to the mean of its group, repeat.

LINEAR REGRESSION

What percent is damaged?

It fits a line that predicts a number. For example, the percent of leaf damage from the area of the spots.

LOGISTIC REGRESSION

Healthy or sick?

It gives a probability between 0 and 1. If it is above 0.5, the leaf is sick.

KNN

Which disease is it?

K-Nearest Neighbors. It finds the K most similar leaves it knows and chooses the disease that appears most.

Example with K = 5: if 4 of the 5 nearest leaves have rust level 1, the answer is rust level 1.

Q-LEARNING

What is the best action?

An agent learns by trying actions and getting rewards. A table (Q-table) stores how good each action is in each state.

It also thinks about the future, not only the next reward. In this project, the state is the diagnosis and the actions are things like “keep watching” or “apply treatment.” I will define the rewards as simple rules in Phase 3.
